How to Transfer Google Authenticator to a New Phone — Without Locking Yourself Out

Updated 2026-11 · 7 min read

Contacts sync themselves; your two-factor accounts do not. Unless you enabled cloud sync, every TOTP secret inside Google Authenticator lives only on the old phone — a naive "just switch phones" leaves all of your 2FA behind. This guide gives you three routes, ordered from best to worst case.

Step 0: Identify your situation

SituationTestRoute
Both phones in handBoth unlock, the app opens on bothRoute 1: official export/import
Only the old phone worksNew phone is fresh; old app opensRoute 2: export-code relay
Old phone lost or deadOld device won't unlock or power onRoute 3: per-service recovery

Route 1: Both phones in hand (official export/import)

  1. On the old phone: Google Authenticator → ⋮ menu → Export accounts
  2. Select the accounts (multi-select supported) → Next — a QR code appears
  3. On the new phone: install Google Authenticator → ⋮ menu → Import accounts
  4. Scan the QR with the new phone → verify every account produces codes
  5. Only after verification, decide whether to remove data from the old phone

The official exporter handles roughly 10 accounts per batch — export in several passes if needed. What the export code actually contains is covered in our otpauth-migration export decoding guide.

Route 2: Only the old phone works (export-code relay)

Use this when the new phone will run a different authenticator (Aegis, 2FAS, 1Password…) or you simply want the keys out in a portable format:

  1. Generate the export QR on the old phone (as above) and take a screenshot
  2. Open the 2fa.pub export decoder on any computer, upload the screenshot or press Ctrl+V to paste it
  3. Read out each account's issuer, name, and Base32 secret — decoded locally in your browser, nothing uploaded
  4. On the new phone, add entries manually in your chosen app, or convert secrets back to scannable otpauth:// codes with the 2FA QR generator

The advantage: no account system involved, a universal key format, and no vendor lock-in after the move.

Route 3: The old phone is gone (per-service recovery)

Hard truth first: the authenticator cannot be recovered from the cloud — TOTP secrets existed only on the device. What you can do is reset 2FA service by service:

  • Backup codes: the one-time recovery codes saved at 2FA enrollment — each works once; log in and re-enroll immediately
  • Bound phone/email: most services let you reset 2FA after SMS or email verification
  • Neither: file a manual recovery request with the provider (usually requires identity proof; days to weeks)

This is why "authenticator + backup codes" is the standard pairing — offline backup codes are the only thing that saves you after a lost phone.

Codes wrong after the move? It's the clock, 9 times out of 10

TOTP = secret + current time, in 30-second windows. A new phone with a drifted clock (common right after a backup restore or a manual timezone change) computes codes the server rejects. Set date & time to automatic / network-provided and retry. On iOS you can also run the time-sync correction inside Google Authenticator's settings.

After migrating: build the backup you wish you had

  • Save backup codes for every important service, offline (printed or in a password manager)
  • Export all secrets as a Base32 list via the export decoder and store it in an encrypted vault or password manager
  • Consider switching to an authenticator with encrypted backups (Aegis, 2FAS) so the next move is a restore, not a migration

FAQ

Do the codes on the old phone keep working after migration? Yes — accounts exist on both sides independently and that causes no conflict. Still, once the new phone checks out, delete the data on the old one, especially before selling or giving it away.

I use cloud sync — anything to do? Signing into the same Google account on the new device pulls the synced accounts automatically. Manual export remains the more complete and more private route.

Does this work for banking apps too? Everything that uses standard TOTP (scan-a-QR enrollment) transfers the same way. Some banks and enterprise SSO providers disable exporting — those accounts must be re-enrolled through the provider's own flow.

iPhone to Android (or back)? The export format is identical; cross-platform moves are fine. Route 2 is the most reliable for that.
